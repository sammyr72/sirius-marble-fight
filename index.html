A tiny arena appears on your real table, and two action-figure-sized robots square up in it. You are one of them: your hands are the fists, and every fist is a marble. Swing, block, and knock your rival out of the ring, or dent its head if you hit hard enough. Rounds are quick, you can play from a chair, and the marbles you win are yours to keep. Or hone your mental acuity with an endless supply of wooden marble mazes, or relax with our Zen-like ASMR table. Focus, Fight, & Flow.

How we got here

Eight and a half light-years away is a system of two suns. The big one, Sirius A, is the Dog Star, and it is huge. The small blue one is Sirius B, and it is not. Nine planets circle it, and the ninth is the angry one.

The ninth planet is a world of battle robots. They have never made a thing that wasn’t a weapon. They have a navy, an air force, and a very strong opinion about the Earth.

So the Battle Fleet of the Ninth Planet set out, screaming across the vast empty wastes of interstellar space. Rocket boots on, fists first, eight and a half light-years to go.

TARGET: EARTH!!!

There was a small problem.

Somewhere in the ninth planet’s paperwork, a decimal point had wandered off. The invincible armada was not the size of a skyscraper. It was the size of an action figure.

The flagship fits inside a coffee mug. The whole fleet fits in a shoebox. Their weapons are mostly marbles.

— a note on the ninth planet’s maths: they checked it once, and it said ‘×18’ in the margin.

They lost. We won.

It was not close. We picked them up and put them on the coffee table. Some surrendered on the spot. A few kept their battle poses on for the photographs.

  • Declares victory (too early)
  • Winding up
  • Unimpressed
  • Surrender (or a stretch)

And now we make them fight each other, for our amusement.

They take it very seriously. That’s the best part.

How a fight goes

The short version, in the order it happens.

  1. 1

    Set the table

    The arena sits on your real table in passthrough, about forty centimetres across, with robots at 1:18 scale. A roulette wheel picks the place. Online, both headsets spin the same wheel and land on the same arena.

  2. 2

    Draw two marbles

    Each fighter gets one, drawn from the collection you own. You don’t choose; you fight with what you drew. No two marbles look alike.

  3. 3

    Fight

    The left stick walks your robot. Hold the grip and your hand flies a fist. A faster swing hits harder. Knock the other robot off the table, out of the ring, or out cold.

  4. 4

    First to three

    A marble that loses a round turns to steel. And if it’s lost to a knock out, it goes into the winner’s pool. You win the match online against another player, you keep the marbles, no purchase necessary.

Small things we fussed over

Things nobody asked for. They’re all in.

Dents
The fist pushes the robot’s shell in along its path, so the dent matches the punch.
Weight
The fists are glass, steel, stone, or clay marbles on a tiny metal robot body, so a swing carries its mass and a miss pulls you round with it.
Wobbly heads
Take enough hits to the head and the robot gets woozy. It staggers more and goes down easier.
Slow-mo
When a round ends, time crawls, the room dims and the sound sinks. The camera never moves. It’s your head.
Barks
The robots talk! The voices are synthesized on the spot, and you can use all 175 of them to make your bot talk back.
Footprints
Sand, dust, gravel and clay take a print, sized to the robot. Big Bots crack the concrete.

One robot, many skins

The robot is a faceless, white-panelled toy android, and there are more than seventy skins to dress it in: flags and colours, a tuxedo, a panda, a samurai, mirror chrome, and glowing ones that move: neon veins, circuit traces, molten seams. Skins are bought with SPACEBUCKS, which you earn by fighting. You can top up with real money too, if you’re impatient.

The bots have temperaments. Some box, some shove, and some just lean on you until you run out of table. Every one has a name, and a few are huge.

It isn’t only a brawler

The robots are the main event. The marbles have other plans for your table.

Regular marbles. Online. For keeps.

Ringer is the playground game: a chalk ring, thirteen marbles in a cross, a shooter in your hand, and a surface you have to read. Knock marbles out of the ring and they’re yours.

Play someone online, win, and you can capture their shooter: the big, fat, shiny one they brought to the ring. You get a copy for your collection. They keep theirs, but you’ll both remember.

Tilt the Labyrinth

A wooden maze board on the table, coiled into one long corridor with forty-two numbered gates to pass in order, and holes waiting in between. Tilt it with your hands and roll your marble home.

Steady hands are rewarded. Finish a run and a marble comes out.

Work out in the gym

A heavy bag, a double-end bag and a sparring dummy that stands there and takes it. Warm up, practise your jab, and learn how your swing carries.

It’s also where you get dressed and shop: the locker room for skins, the marble shop, and the bark phone where you choose what your robot says.

NOTHING TO WIN

Flow

Fight is loud. Focus asks for steady hands. Flow is the quiet page of the console, where nothing is trying to knock you over. Stay a while and marbles come out, slowly.

ASMR table

A tray of steel, ivory, stone or hardwood marbles, and the sound of them rolling. Press X to change the material. It’s the quietest thing in the game and it knows it.

Cozy room

Not into passthrough? Swap your room for a softly lit one with a couch, a rug and a window at dusk. The game stays on the table.

Lights

Ten light scenes on a rotary switch. Each one is two slow effects that never quite line up, so the room never repeats itself.

in the works

Sand Tray

A Labyrinth with the labyrinth taken out: no walls, no numbers, no lines, no holes. Just sand, and your big old shooter in the middle of it.

The controls are the same as the Labyrinth. Tilt the tray and the shooter rolls, leaving a slow, crunchy trail behind it. Hands on, haptically supported ASMR joy...

Pick your fight

The old CRT on the table is where you choose. Fight on your own, or take it online.

> SINGLE PLAYER: THE CIRCUIT

A ladder of named bots, from a cautious defender up to a Big Boss. Climb it and the marbles keep coming. Mangle some bots.

> G-G-G-GAUNTLET!!!

Beating the Circuit unlocks The Gauntlet: nine rounds of progressive pugilistic punishment the first time through, then ten more every time you survive it, all the way up to a hundred. Can you make it through?

MULTIPLAYER, online

Face another player. Win marbles, earn SPACEBUCKS, make friends, and mangle each other’s robots. Both headsets stay in sync, so you see the same arena, the same marbles and the same result.

It’s built to be safe and to stay friendly. There are no microphones and no voice chat. You talk with quick, fun, built-in messages that can’t be spammed. Blocking and reporting are there, using the account tools Meta provides.

No ads. No analytics. The privacy policy is short and says what’s kept.
